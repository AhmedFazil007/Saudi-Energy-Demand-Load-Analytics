Retail Sales & Profitability

A profitability case study connecting product mix, discount bands, channels and estimated gross margin.

30KTransactions
SAR 19.62MModeled revenue
26.93%Estimated gross margin

The business question

Are the products driving revenue also protecting profit?

How I approached it

  • Analyzed 30,000 simulated transactions across 2023–2025.
  • Compared category revenue, estimated gross profit, discounts and channel performance.
  • Separated topline sales from estimated profitability when interpreting the results.

Key findings

  • Electronics contributed about SAR 14.94M, or 76.1% of revenue.
  • Transactions without discounts had an estimated gross margin of 32.93%, compared with 18.94% for discounts above 10%.
  • Revenue was nearly evenly split between branches (50.4%) and e-commerce (49.6%).

Why it matters

A high-revenue category can create concentration risk, while discounting deserves evaluation against gross profit rather than sales volume alone. The discount comparison is observational, not causal.

Recommendations I'd explore

  • Compare product-level margins, inventory turnover and stock availability.
  • Review promotional results within comparable product groups instead of applying blanket discount cuts.
  • Assess channel contribution after delivery, marketing and operating costs.

How I'd measure potential impact

  • Estimated gross profit by product
  • Gross margin by discount band
  • Inventory turnover and channel contribution

These are suggested measures for future testing, not results from implemented changes.

Scope and limitations

Gross profit is estimated, not audited. The modeled discount bands do not prove discounts caused the margin difference.

This independent project uses synthetic data. The figures are not official Saudi statistics, and no financial or operational improvement is claimed as achieved.

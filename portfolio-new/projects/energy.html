Energy Demand & Load Patterns

A demand-analysis exercise combining city, customer sector, temperature and daily consumption.

The business question

What do seasonal and sector patterns reveal beyond annual energy totals?

How I approached it

  • Analyzed 58,440 simulated daily city-sector observations across 2022–2025.
  • Compared energy consumption by city, customer sector and season.
  • Distinguished consumption measured in MWh from power demand measured in MW.

Key findings

  • Industrial use contributed about 8.94M MWh and residential use about 7.86M MWh.
  • Riyadh represented around 3.94M MWh of modeled consumption.
  • Summer showed the highest average consumption per record, around 494.55 MWh.

Why it matters

Fairly stable annual totals can hide meaningful differences in sector demand and seasonality. Consumption totals should not be confused with grid peak load.

Recommendations I'd explore

  • Build monthly and seasonal demand profiles for major customer sectors.
  • Investigate weather-normalized patterns using verified meter readings.
  • Validate peak-load and forecast methods before considering capacity changes.

How I'd measure potential impact

  • Energy consumption (MWh)
  • Measured coincident peak load (MW)
  • Forecast error by season and sector

These are suggested measures for future testing, not results from implemented changes.

Scope and limitations

The reported 2,059 MW maximum is a single-record value, not a simultaneous grid peak. Renewable-share inputs are modeled assumptions.

This independent project uses synthetic data. The figures are not official Saudi statistics, and no financial or operational improvement is claimed as achieved.

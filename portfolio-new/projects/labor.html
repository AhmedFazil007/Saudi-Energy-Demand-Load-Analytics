Labor Market & Hiring Demand

A labor-market reporting exercise distinguishing vacancy volume from vacancy intensity.

13,826Modeled Q4 vacancies
5.30%Vacancy-to-employment ratio
8.48%IT vacancy ratio

The business question

Which sectors show the strongest hiring demand relative to their size?

How I approached it

  • Organized simulated labor observations across ten cities, eight sectors and 28 quarters.
  • Compared absolute vacancies with vacancies divided by employment.
  • Used employment-weighted salary measures for comparisons across differently sized groups.

Key findings

  • Wholesale & Retail had 2,317 modeled vacancies in 2025 Q4, the largest sector count.
  • Information Technology had the highest sector vacancy-to-employment ratio at 8.48%.
  • Riyadh led city vacancy volume, while Madinah had the highest city-level vacancy ratio at 5.60%.

Why it matters

Large sectors can lead in openings without having the highest relative demand. Both absolute volume and ratios are needed for a useful hiring discussion.

Recommendations I'd explore

  • Use both vacancy count and vacancy rate when shortlisting sectors.
  • Validate simulated demand against current job postings and official statistics.
  • Keep demographic visuals and headline employment KPIs on the same quarter.

How I'd measure potential impact

  • Vacancies by sector and city
  • Vacancy-to-employment ratio
  • Employment-weighted salary

These are suggested measures for future testing, not results from implemented changes.

Scope and limitations

The existing source dashboard's demographic donut charts aggregate across quarters, unlike the latest-quarter headline KPI. These should not be interpreted as matching current headcounts.

This independent project uses synthetic data. The figures are not official Saudi statistics, and no financial or operational improvement is claimed as achieved.

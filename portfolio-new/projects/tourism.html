HOSPITALITY / REVENUE · Independent case study

Tourism & Hospitality Performance

A city-level comparison of visitor demand, accommodation revenue, room pricing and occupancy.

840City-month observations
3.54MModeled 2025 visitors
SAR 1.76BModeled 2025 room revenue

The business question

What happens to accommodation performance when visitor demand changes?

How I approached it

  • Prepared 840 simulated monthly observations across ten cities from 2019–2025.
  • Compared year-over-year visitor demand and accommodation revenue.
  • Used capacity-weighted occupancy and average daily rate rather than simple averages.

Key findings

  • In 2025 visitors fell around 11.5%, while accommodation revenue declined around 9.4%.
  • Modeled revenue per visitor increased from SAR 485 to SAR 496.
  • Capacity-weighted occupancy shifted from 57.7% in 2024 to 56.8% in 2025.

Why it matters

Visitor counts, occupancy and pricing tell different parts of the story. A hotel group should not infer commercial performance from arrivals alone.

Recommendations I'd explore

  • Review occupancy and revenue by city and month to locate demand gaps.
  • Compare ADR with occupancy before changing prices.
  • Test targeted campaigns in weaker periods and compare results with a defined baseline.

How I'd measure potential impact

  • Occupancy rate
  • Average daily rate (ADR)
  • Accommodation revenue and revenue per available room

These are suggested measures for future testing, not results from implemented changes.

Scope and limitations

Revenue per visitor is a ratio of simulated totals, not measured guest spending. The data is not official tourism reporting.

This independent project uses synthetic data. The figures are not official Saudi statistics, and no financial or operational improvement is claimed as achieved.
